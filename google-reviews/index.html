Google Reviews

How to ask for Google reviews the right way

Reviews influence both customer trust and your visibility — but Google has strict rules about how you're allowed to collect them. Here's how to do it well and stay compliant.

Why reviews matter

Reviews are one of the signals that feed into how prominent your business looks in local search and Maps results, and they're often the first thing a new customer reads before deciding to visit. A profile with a handful of old reviews tends to look less trustworthy than one with a steady, recent flow — regardless of the star average.

Compliant ways to ask for reviews

  • Ask in person or via a direct message, after a genuinely completed purchase or service — a simple, polite request works better than a generic mass message.
  • Share your review link (from your Business Profile) on receipts, invoices, or a WhatsApp follow-up, so customers can leave a review with minimal friction.
  • Ask everyone the same way — don't selectively ask only happy-looking customers and skip unhappy ones; that's considered review-gating and violates Google's policies.
  • Time it well — right after a positive interaction is usually more effective than days later.
Avoid: offering discounts, cash, or gifts in exchange for reviews (incentivized reviews), asking staff or family to post reviews, or filtering who gets asked based on how happy they seemed. These violate Google's review policies and can lead to reviews being removed or your profile being penalized.

Responding to reviews

Respond to reviews — both positive and negative — in a calm, specific, non-defensive tone. A short, genuine thank-you works for positive reviews. For negative ones, acknowledge the issue, avoid arguing publicly, and where appropriate, invite the person to continue the conversation privately.

  • Never post a fake positive review about your own business, or ask someone to remove a truthful negative one in exchange for something
  • Don't copy-paste the same generic reply to every review — specific responses read as more genuine
  • If a review clearly violates Google's policies (e.g. it's abusive, fake, or off-topic), you can flag it for review through your Business Profile rather than responding publicly
