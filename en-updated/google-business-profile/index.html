How to set up and optimize your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile (GBP) — sometimes still called "Google My Business" — is usually the first thing a customer sees about you on Google. Here's what it is, how to set it up correctly, and what "optimized" actually means.

What is a Google Business Profile?

A Google Business Profile is the free listing that shows up when someone searches for your business name, or for the kind of business you run, on Google Search and Google Maps. It typically shows your name, category, address, hours, phone number, photos, and reviews.

It's separate from your website — you can have a strong Business Profile even before you have a website, which is why it's usually the best starting point for local visibility.

Setting up your profile correctly

  • Business name: use your real, registered business name — not keywords stuffed in (e.g. "Sharma Sweets Best Sweets Shop Delhi"), which violates Google's guidelines and can get a listing suspended.
  • Category: choose the most specific category that matches what you do. This affects which searches you show up for.
  • Address and service area: set this accurately — incorrect addresses are one of the most common reasons profiles get suspended or hidden.
  • Hours: keep regular and holiday hours current. Missing or wrong hours are a common source of customer frustration.
  • Verification: Google typically verifies new listings by postcard, phone, email, or video, depending on your business type and location. This step is required before your listing is fully visible.
Good to know: Verification method and timelines are set by Google and can vary — check the options shown inside your own Business Profile setup rather than assuming one method applies to you.

What "optimized" means in practice

Optimization isn't a trick — it's completeness and accuracy. Profiles that are more complete tend to give customers (and Google) clearer signals about what the business does and where it operates. In practice, that usually means:

  • A clear, well-lit logo and cover photo, plus photos of your space, products, or work
  • A business description written in plain language, without keyword stuffing
  • Attributes filled in where relevant (e.g. wheelchair accessible, accepts online payments)
  • Questions & Answers section kept accurate, since anyone can post here

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Creating duplicate listings for the same location — this splits your reviews and confuses customers
  • Using a virtual office or PO box address for a business type that requires a physical location
  • Leaving the profile unmanaged after verification — outdated hours and no responses to questions or reviews reduce trust
