Google Business Profile Audit Checklist: 40 Things to Check

Audit your own profile in an hour. Work from top to bottom, note what is wrong, fix the basics first and leave the nice-to-haves for later.

Before you start

Open your profile while signed in as an owner or manager, then open the public view on your phone in a private window to see what customers see. Keep a notepad open. For each item below, mark it as fine, needs fixing or not applicable. This audit is about accuracy and completeness. Fixing these items makes you eligible to be found for the right searches, but nobody can promise a ranking.

If you do not yet have a profile, start with how to set up and verify your profile. If your profile has been suspended, read the guide on suspensions on our blog first.

1. Ownership and verification

  • The profile is verified and shows no warnings.
  • At least two trusted people have access, and one is an owner with a business email.
  • There is only one profile for each physical location; search for duplicates by address and phone.
  • Old managers and agencies no longer needed have been removed.

2. Business name, address and contact

  • The name matches your signboard, without keywords, area names or slogans added.
  • The address is complete and formatted the way a local would write it, with a landmark in the description or photos if needed.
  • The pin is on the entrance, not 200 metres away.
  • Service-area businesses hide their home address and list areas actually served.
  • The main phone number is answered during the hours shown; WhatsApp is on your website if you use it.
  • The website link opens fast on mobile and shows the same name, address and phone.

3. Categories

  • The primary category is the most specific one that matches your core business.
  • Secondary categories are only for services you genuinely offer.
  • Categories on the profile match your website and listings elsewhere.

4. Hours

  • Regular hours, weekly off and any lunch break are correct.
  • Special hours for upcoming festivals and holidays have been set.
  • Online-only or by-appointment service is stated clearly.

5. Services, products and description

  • Every real service or product is listed with a one-line description and, where sensible, a starting price.
  • The description tells people who you are, what you do and where, in plain language, without links, offers or phone numbers.
  • Attributes (delivery, parking, accessibility, payment options) are selected only where true.
  • Menu, booking and appointment links work.

Use the description generator and services generator to draft text, then edit it to reflect your real business.

6. Photos and videos

  • There is a clear exterior photo from the street, an entrance photo and several interior photos.
  • Team, products or work photos are recent and real; no stock images or heavy filters.
  • Photos are added every month or two, and any outdated ones are removed.
  • Customer-uploaded photos are checked, and any inappropriate ones are reported.

7. Reviews

  • You can open your review link and QR code and it works. The QR generator can help.
  • All reviews have a reply, written in a calm, specific and brief manner.
  • You ask every customer, not only happy ones, and never offer rewards or filter who can review.
  • Suspicious or policy-violating reviews have been reported through the proper channel; see the review dispute helper for drafting.
  • You use the reply generator only as a starting point, not as copy-paste.

8. Posts, Q&A and messages

  • Recent posts exist, with real photos, within the last month.
  • Questions in the Q&A section have been answered.
  • Messaging, if available for you, is answered promptly.

9. Policy risks

  • No keyword stuffing in name, description or posts.
  • No misleading claims such as guaranteed results or 'best in India'.
  • No fake reviews, no incentives, no gating.
  • A virtual office or PO box address is not used as the location.

Check Google's current Business Profile guidelines, because they change. If you manage regulated services such as healthcare, legal or financial, follow your regulator's advertising rules too.

10. Interpreting what you find

Sort your notes into three piles: fix today, fix this month and nice to have. Most profiles improve most from accurate categories, complete services, recent real photos and replies to reviews. After a month, look at your profile's calls and direction requests, and use rank tracking to see whether visibility around your area has changed. A 60-minute plan for the audit

  • Minutes 0 to 10: open the public view on your phone and note what a customer sees in the first five seconds.
  • Minutes 10 to 25: check name, address, pin, phone, categories and hours against the signboard and your website.
  • Minutes 25 to 40: review services, description, attributes and photos; list what is missing.
  • Minutes 40 to 55: read the last ten reviews and your replies; test your review link; check Q&A and posts.
  • Minutes 55 to 60: sort the list into fix today, this month and later.

Repeat the quick version each month. Small, regular fixes keep a profile accurate in a way that a big annual clean-up cannot.

What an auditor should not tell you

Be careful with anyone who finds fifty "critical" problems and sells a fix, or promises first place after an audit. Real audits are usually short: a few inaccurate details, some missing services and photos, a review habit to build. Ask any auditor to show evidence for each finding and to explain why it matters.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Treating the audit as a one-time exercise.
  • Adding categories you hope to rank for but do not serve.
  • Stuffing the name with keywords or area names.
  • Forgetting festival timings.
  • Ignoring negative reviews.
  • Skipping the check for duplicates.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I audit my Business Profile?

Do a full audit every quarter and a quick check monthly for hours, photos and reviews. Check again after any change of address, phone number or services.

Can an audit guarantee a higher ranking?

No. It removes errors and gaps that can hold you back, but ranking depends on relevance, distance, prominence and competition, which nobody can guarantee.

What if I find a duplicate profile?

Do not create a new one. Use Google's tools to request a merge or removal, or claim the duplicate if it is yours, and keep your details consistent.

Can I run this audit without paid software?

Yes. Everything here can be done by hand with your own profile. Software only speeds up checking and tracking.
