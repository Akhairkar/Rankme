Competitor analysis for local businesses

Knowing who ranks around you helps you decide what to fix first. LocalBoost compares you with a handful of nearby competitors using facts you can check yourself.

AvailabilityThe manual comparison tool is free. In the paid product, saved competitors can be matched to Google Places listings that you confirm yourself. That needs a Google Maps key on the server and the production API, which are not connected to this website yet, so it is launching with Pro.

What competitor analysis is for

Search results are relative. If three nearby clinics each have two hundred reviews and you have twelve, that gap is more useful to know than any abstract score. Competitor analysis gives you a short, honest list of where you are behind, where you are level and where you already lead.

What can be compared

  • Review count and average rating. Public numbers that anyone can read on a listing.
  • Primary and secondary categories. Often the quickest win to spot when a competitor uses a more specific category.
  • Profile completeness. Whether hours, description, services, photos and a website are present.
  • Website basics. Whether the competitor has a title and heading that mention the service and city, a mobile-friendly page and local business structured data. We can read these from public pages.
  • Rank on your grid. Once rank tracking is live, how often a competitor appears at the points where you do not.

How you add competitors

In the free comparison tool you type in the figures for two or more businesses you looked up yourself, and the tool lines them up. Nothing is scraped from Google on your behalf.

In the paid product you add a competitor by name and website. Details you type are saved as user entered and unverified, and they are never shown as verified. You can ask the server to look the business up in the official Google Places API. It returns candidate matches and you choose the right one yourself; nothing is auto-selected. Only then are the rating and review count stored, together with the source and the time they were fetched. The number of saved competitors depends on your plan: none on Free, five on Pro and twenty-five on Agency at the planned launch limits, which the pricing page will confirm.

What we deliberately do not do

We do not scrape private data, create fake accounts, or post anything about a competitor. We do not tell you to report a competitor's listing unless it clearly breaks Google's rules, and we explain that process in our review guides. We also do not estimate a competitor's revenue, visitors or ad spend, because we cannot measure those honestly.

Limitations

  • Public numbers lag. A competitor's recent reviews may not be reflected yet.
  • Two businesses in the same category can serve different customers, so a raw comparison needs your judgement.
  • Competitor data tells you what exists, not why it works. Use it to choose experiments rather than to copy.

Where to start

Try the free competitor comparison tool with two or three real competitors. Then run the free business audit on yourself so you have the same figures for your own listing. If you want saved competitors and automatic updates, see the Pro plan and join the waitlist.

Frequently asked questions

Do you scrape Google to get competitor data?
No. The free tool uses figures you enter. In the paid product, rating and review count come only from the official Google Places API after you confirm a match, and manual details stay labelled as unverified.
How many competitors can I track?
The limit depends on your plan and is shown on the pricing page. Free accounts use the manual tool instead.
Can this tell me why a competitor ranks higher?
It shows differences. It cannot prove the cause, because Google does not publish its ranking formula.
