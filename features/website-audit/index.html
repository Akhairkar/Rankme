Website audit: what we check, how, and what we cannot know

A website audit reads your public pages the way a visitor and a search crawler would, then lists specific problems with the evidence we found. It does not predict where you will rank.

AvailabilityA one-page check is the free tier. Multi-page crawling is part of the Pro plan, which is launching: join the waitlist at the bottom of this page. The production API that runs the crawler is not connected to this website yet.

What the audit looks at

The crawler requests the page you give it and reads the HTML that comes back. For each page it records the HTTP status, the title tag, the meta description, how many H1 headings there are, the canonical link, robots directives, whether the page is indexable, the visible word count, images that have no alt text, internal and broken links, structured data types such as LocalBusiness, and whether a mobile viewport tag exists. These are plain facts about the page, so they are marked as measured.

On a paid plan the crawler follows internal links up to a page limit that depends on the plan, and reads your robots.txt and sitemap first so it respects what you have asked crawlers to skip. The free tier checks a single page.

How the score works

Each check has a weight. Checks are grouped into sections. A check can pass, fail, or be unavailable when the data could not be read. Unavailable checks are left out of the calculation rather than counted as failures, and if too little could be checked the audit shows no score at all instead of inventing one. The score describes the health and completeness of the things we inspected. It is not a prediction of Google rankings, and we do not claim that any single check is a confirmed ranking factor. Read understanding your audit score for the full method.

What a finding contains

Every finding has a severity, a short title, the evidence we saw (for example the exact title text, or the URL that returned a 404), the source of that evidence, why it matters to a customer or a crawler, a recommended action and plain instructions. Findings can be marked open, fixed or ignored, and two audits can be compared to show what is new and what you fixed. An audit can be exported as CSV.

Safety of the crawler

The crawler is designed to be safe to expose publicly. It refuses private, loopback and internal network addresses, limits redirects and response sizes, and applies a rate limit per visitor. You can only audit URLs that are reachable from the public internet, which also means pages behind a login or a firewall cannot be checked.

Limitations

  • We read the HTML the server returns. Content that appears only after heavy JavaScript runs may not be seen.
  • A pass on every check does not mean you will rank well; competition, distance and relevance matter and are outside what a website audit can see.
  • We do not read your Search Console data in the audit unless you connect it separately and grant permission.
  • Speed numbers are only shown when a PageSpeed key is configured on the server; otherwise you will see that they are unavailable.

Frequently asked questions

Does the audit change anything on my website?
No. The crawler only reads public pages. It never logs in, submits forms or edits your site.
Can the audit promise better rankings?
No. It lists problems and how to fix them. Rankings depend on many things we cannot control, so we never guarantee a position.
Why does my audit show no score?
If too few checks could be run, for example because the site blocked our crawler, we show the findings we have and leave the score empty instead of guessing.
