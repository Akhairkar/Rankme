Understanding your audit score

Sections, weights, missing data and what the score means.

Last updated 30 October 2026

AvailabilityThe scoring model below is the one in the audit engine code. It runs in the free audit and, once the production API is online, in the saved audits for paid plans.

What the score is

The score is a number from 0 to 100 that summarises how complete, consistent and technically sound the information you listed is. It is not a prediction of your Google ranking, and none of the checks are claimed to be confirmed Google ranking factors. Each check explains the practical effect on customers or crawlers instead.

How it is calculated

Every check has a weight. Checks are grouped into sections, and a section's weight is the sum of its check weights. A check can be:

  • Pass: counts its full weight.
  • Fail: counts zero but stays in the total.
  • Unavailable: the data could not be read. It is left out of the calculation entirely.

For each section: section score = points earned divided by points available. The overall score is the weighted average of the sections that had any available checks, rounded to a whole number. If the available checks add up to less than 40 percent of the total weight, the audit shows no score and the state is partial. We prefer an empty score to an invented one.

The sections and their weights

Weights can change when the model version changes; the version number is stored with every audit. Response time is displayed but carries no weight.

Provided by you versus verified

Without a Google connection, profile checks use what you entered and are labelled as provided by you. The consistency and review sections need Google data, so without a connection they are unavailable and drop out of the score. That is why a score without a Google connection can look higher or lower than one with it. Compare scores only within the same state.

Why your score might change when nothing changed

  • Some checks moved from unavailable to available after you connected Google or fixed a blocked page.
  • The model version changed.
  • You added or removed reviews.

Check the evidence on each finding to see what moved.

How to use the score

Use it as a to-do list ranking, not a grade. A score of 70 with the right five items fixed is better than a score of 90 on items that do not matter to your customers. Track the trend across audits, and focus on findings marked high severity.

What the score does not include

Photos, posts, questions and answers, backlinks, citations on other sites, competitor strength, your distance from the searcher and your real-world reputation. These matter and some are covered by other features, but they are not in this number.

Scores in reports

Reports show the score, the previous score and the change, together with the model note that the score is not a ranking prediction. If a previous score is missing, the change is shown as unavailable.

A worked example

Suppose a salon has no Google connection and only entered its details. The profile section has 25 points available and the salon passes checks worth 20 of them, so that section scores 80 percent. The website section has 25 available and 15 are earned, so 60 percent. Consistency and reviews are unavailable and drop out. Local landing pages score 100 percent on 5 available points and schema scores zero on 10. The weighted average uses only the sections that had checks: (25 x 0.8 + 25 x 0.6 + 5 x 1 + 10 x 0) divided by (25 + 25 + 5 + 10), which is 40 divided by 65, so about 62. After connecting Google, the other sections join the calculation and the number can change even though nothing on the website did.

Frequently asked questions

Is a higher score a higher Google ranking?
No. The score measures completeness and consistency of your information. Rankings depend on many other factors.
Why is there no score?
Fewer than 40 percent of the weighted checks could be run, so we show findings without a number.
Can I reach 100?
Yes if every available check passes, but that does not guarantee any ranking or traffic.
