Rank tracking guide

Keywords, grid scans, reading positions, limits.

Last updated 30 October 2026

AvailabilityParts of this guide describe the signed-in product. They become available when the production API is online. That API is not connected to this website yet, so today only the free tools work without an account. Rank scans also need a licensed rank-data provider, which is not connected yet. Until then a scan returns an explicit "awaiting provider" state.

What a scan is

A scan asks a rank-data provider where your business appears in local results for one keyword, at several points arranged in a grid around your business. You get one position per point. This shows how your visibility changes with distance, which a single number cannot.

Choosing keywords

Pick the phrases a real customer would type, with the service and sometimes the place, such as "dentist near me" or "hair salon in Indiranagar". Start with three to five. Plans limit the number of tracked keywords. Avoid brand-new keyword lists of fifty items; more data is not more insight if you cannot act on it.

Choosing grid size and radius

  • 3 x 3 is quick and uses the fewest scans. Good for a first look.
  • 5 x 5 shows more detail for a medium-sized area.
  • 7 x 7 is the most detailed and uses the most scans.

The radius is the distance from the centre to the edge in metres. Use a smaller radius for walk-in businesses in dense areas, and a larger one for businesses that travel to customers. Your plan has a monthly scan allowance, so the first scans are worth planning.

Reading the result

  • A number is your position at that point. Lower is better.
  • An empty point means you were not found in the results at that point. It is not a zero and not a guess.
  • Average position is the mean of the points where you were found, shown alongside how many points that was.
  • Visibility summarises how much of the grid you appear in.
  • If the provider failed for some points, the scan is marked partial and says so.

Because averages hide gaps, look at the map first and the average second.

Scheduling and history

A scan can be repeated on a schedule so you can see movement over weeks. Compare scans with the same keyword, grid size and radius only. Changing any of them makes the comparison meaningless. Scans can be exported as CSV.

What usually moves local results

These are general practices, with no guarantees: a complete and accurate profile with the right primary category, consistent name, address and phone across the web, a steady flow of genuine reviews with replies, a website that clearly mentions your service and area, and real proximity to the searcher. Rankings can also change because competitors change. Read the Google Maps SEO guides for the how-to.

Limits you should know

  • Provider data is a sample from a moment in time and from one kind of device. Customers may see something different, because Google personalises results.
  • A scan does not tell you why a position changed.
  • Grids cannot cover every street.
  • No tool, including this one, can guarantee a position.

If a scan does not run

Check whether you have scan allowance left this month, whether your plan includes rank tracking, and whether the scan state says awaiting provider or provider error. If it says awaiting provider, the service is not connected to a data source yet. The rank grid simulator lets you see how a grid looks while you wait; its data is illustrative.

Frequently asked questions

What does an empty grid point mean?
Your business did not appear in the results returned for that location. It is stored empty, never as a guess.
Why is the rank different from what I see on my phone?
Results depend on location, device, time and personalisation. A scan is a sample, not what every customer sees.
How often should I scan?
Weekly or monthly is enough for most small businesses. More frequent scans mostly add noise and use your allowance.
