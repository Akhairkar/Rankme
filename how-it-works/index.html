How LocalBoost works

From adding your business to a report you can check: the six steps, the data we read, what is measured and what is not, and where our limits are.

Status: the free tools are live. Connected features are part of Pro and Agency, launching soon.

How it works

The six steps

Step by step

1

Add your business

You give LocalBoost the basics: business name, category, city and website. On Pro you add one business; on Agency up to 10.

Available in: Free tools: you type the business name into the visibility check.

2

Connect your data

You sign in with Google and approve access to your Business Profile. Access is read-only unless you confirm a specific action. You can revoke it any time from your Google Account.

Available in: Pro and Agency, launching soon.

3

Audit

LocalBoost reviews the profile, the website and the reviews against a checklist. For each issue it records what it saw, where and when.

Available in: Free tools: you answer 8 questions or work through the checklist. Pro: automated, on live data.

4

Fix

Findings become an ordered action list. Each action explains what to change and why. Nothing on your Google profile is changed unless you confirm it.

Available in: Free tools: guided checklists and generators for descriptions, services and review replies.

5

Track

Over time LocalBoost records reviews, positions for keywords and locations you choose, and the competitors you pick. Positions are recorded only when a supported search-data provider is connected.

Available in: Pro and Agency, launching soon.

6

Report

A monthly report lists what changed, the evidence for it and what remains open. Agency reports can carry your own branding.

Available in: Pro and Agency, launching soon.

Limitations

Local search is not fully predictable. Here is what we cannot do.

  • Rankings vary. Google results change with the searcher’s location, device, time and history. A rank check is a snapshot, not a permanent position.
  • We do not control Google. We cannot promise a ranking, and following a recommendation does not guarantee a result.
  • We only see what is exposed. We can read data Google and our providers make available. We cannot see competitors’ private data, and some metrics may be limited or unavailable.
  • Small numbers are noisy. With few reviews or searches, changes can be chance rather than a trend.
  • Access can change. Google may change or restrict its programmes. If data is unavailable, the product says so instead of estimating silently.
  • Recommendations are guidance. They follow published Google guidelines and common practice, but you are responsible for the information on your profile.
