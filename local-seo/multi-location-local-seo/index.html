Multi-Location Local SEO: Managing Several Branches Without the Mess

Two shops, ten clinics or fifty franchise outlets all need the same thing: each location is treated as its own business, with consistent branding and clearly separate details.

When does a location deserve its own profile?

As a general rule, a Business Profile is for a business that has a physical location staffed during stated hours where customers can meet you, or that serves customers at their locations within a defined area. If you have three showrooms with their own staff and addresses, each should have its own profile. A warehouse that customers do not visit, a P.O. box, a shared desk or a virtual office generally does not qualify, and creating profiles for those can lead to suspension.

Do not make separate profiles for each service or each doctor in the same clinic if they work from one address under one business, unless Google's guidelines allow it for your situation, for example practitioners with their own distinct practices. Check the current Business Profile guidelines before creating anything.

Keep names, categories and details consistent

  • Use the real business name on all branches. If the signboard says "Sharma Opticals - Andheri", follow Google's naming rules: a location name or area should only be added if it is part of your real, consistent public name.
  • Choose categories that match what that specific branch does. A branch without a pharmacy should not carry a pharmacy category.
  • Give each branch its own local phone number (a number that reaches that outlet) and its own address, hours and photos.
  • Maintain a central sheet with the master details of every location, and update it first when something changes.

See local citations and NAP consistency for how to keep these details aligned across directories.

Create a location page for each branch

Your website should have a page per branch: address, map embed, phone number, timings, services available there, parking and landmarks, staff or manager, and photos of that outlet. Make each page genuinely different and useful, written for the people who will visit that branch. Avoid copying the same text with the city name swapped, which helps nobody and reads as spam.

Link the profile to the matching page, not to the home page. Use the Maps embed tool to add a map, and keep a short list of branches that links to every location page.

Reviews belong to each branch

Customers review a specific outlet. Make sure your review links and QR codes point to the right profile; the QR generator can create one per branch. Train each branch to ask every customer, reply to reviews in their own name and escalate complaints to the manager. Never gate reviews or offer rewards. Compare branches fairly: a new outlet with ten reviews cannot be judged against a ten-year-old one.

If one branch suffers repeated low ratings, treat it as an operational signal first. Fix the cause (queue times, stock, staff behaviour) before changing any marketing.

Ownership, access and bulk management

  • Create a business account or organisation and add managers by their own Google accounts, with the least access they need.
  • Keep at least two trusted owners so that one resignation does not lock you out.
  • Never share a personal password. Remove access when staff leave.
  • For many locations, Google offers bulk tools such as spreadsheet uploads; check which are available for your account type.
  • Use a franchise or agency model only if the owner of every profile remains the business.

Measuring each location

Report per branch: calls, direction requests, new reviews, rating, and the main search terms. A rank grid scan around each outlet will look different because each has different neighbours and competitors. Do not try to make all branches look the same; compare each against its own past and its own market. For a simple one-page format, see local SEO reporting for owners.

Avoiding the common traps

Franchise chains often see duplicate profiles created by customers or old owners. Search for each address regularly, claim or flag duplicates through Google's tools and keep branch managers informed. When a branch closes or moves, mark it as closed or update it, rather than leaving a ghost listing. When two branches are close together, avoid competing against yourself with identical text or photos; give each its own reality.

A worked example (illustrative)

A diagnostics brand has four collection centres in Lucknow, Kanpur and Prayagraj. Each has one master row with address, hours, phone and manager. Each has a page with a map, the tests available at that centre, home-collection areas and photos of the entrance. Each Business Profile points to its own page, and each centre has a QR code on the billing desk linked to that centre's review page. Reports are monthly and per centre. This is an invented setup meant to show a clean structure, not a real business.

When the Kanpur centre moves, the owner updates the master row, then the Google profile, then the page, then the directories, then asks staff to tell callers about the move. Because everything started from one sheet, nothing is missed.

Training the people at each branch

Most multi-location problems are human, not technical. Give each manager a one-page routine: reply to reviews within two days, update special hours before holidays, upload new photos monthly, answer Q&A, and tell head office about duplicates or wrong details. Review the routine every quarter, and recognise branches that follow it. Do not give a single person the power to rename or delete a profile without a second approval.

Multi-location checklist

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Creating profiles for virtual offices or storage locations.
  • Copying the same text on every branch page.
  • Using one phone number for every branch.
  • Ignoring duplicates created by others.
  • Leaving closed branches live.
  • Giving staff the owner's personal login.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use one phone number for all branches?

It is better for each profile to show a number that reaches that location. A central call centre can work if it answers for the branch, but local customers prefer a local line.

How do I handle a franchise?

Each outlet needs its own accurate profile, and the profile should be managed by someone who can control its details. Check Google's guidelines for franchises and keep brand name rules consistent.

Should I add the area name to the business name?

Only if it is part of your real, consistently used public name. Adding keywords or areas that are not part of the name breaks Google's guidelines.

Is a bigger chain always better ranked?

No. Local results depend on relevance, distance and prominence for each search. A single well-run outlet can outperform a chain's branch nearby.
