Local Keyword Research for India: Find What Your Customers Really Type

Good local keyword research is mostly listening: to customers, to staff and to Google's own suggestions. Here is a practical way to build a short list in Hinglish, Hindi and English.

What "local keywords" really are

A local keyword is the phrase a person types or speaks when they want something nearby. It usually has three parts: the thing ("ac repair"), the place ("in Andheri West", "near Hazratganj") and sometimes a modifier ("24 hours", "cheap", "for women", "on Sunday"). Many searches never name the place at all, such as "dentist near me", because Google already knows roughly where the person is.

For a small business, keywords are not a list to stuff into your profile. They are a guide to which services to describe, which words to use on your website, and which questions to answer.

Step 1: Start with your own customers

The best source costs nothing. Write down the first sentence customers use on the phone or in WhatsApp: "bhaiya, mixer ki jar ka repair hota hai?", "sasta aur accha tutor chahiye Class 10 ke liye". Ask your front-desk staff, delivery boys and technicians for the five most common questions. Check your WhatsApp Business labels and call notes for repeats. These are real phrases, often not in any keyword tool.

Step 2: Use Google's own suggestions

  • Type your service into Google or Maps and note the suggestions that appear as you type, then add a letter of the alphabet after it.
  • Look at the "People also ask" box and the "related searches" at the bottom of the results.
  • Search from a phone, with and without the place name, in English, Hindi script and Hinglish spelling, because people type all three.
  • Open the profiles of two or three nearby competitors and read their categories, services and the words customers use in reviews.

The Local Keyword Ideas tool on LocalBoost can help you brainstorm combinations of services and areas. Treat its output as ideas to test, not as search-volume data.

Step 3: Handle Hinglish, Hindi and spelling variants

Indian searches mix languages. "Kundli matching", "कुंडली मिलान" and "kundali milan" are the same need. "Ac service", "AC repair" and "air conditioner servicing" overlap. You do not need a page for each spelling. Use the most natural wording in your main text, include common alternates once where they read naturally, and let Google connect them. If you serve a Hindi-speaking area, a Hindi version of your page or a few Hindi service names in your profile is worth the effort.

Step 4: Add places the way people say them

Customers use neighbourhoods, market names, landmarks, metro stations and PIN codes, not municipal wards. List the real localities you serve, such as "Salt Lake Sector V", "Viman Nagar" or "Sector 62 Noida", on your website's service area section and in posts where it is natural. Avoid pasting fifty place names in one paragraph; that reads like spam and does not help anyone.

Step 5: Build a simple keyword sheet

Use a spreadsheet with four columns: the phrase, the intent (call now, compare, learn), the page or profile section where you answer it, and a note on whether you actually offer it. Group phrases by service. A typical sheet for a clinic might have 20 to 30 rows, not 300. For each row ask: do I offer this, and can I show proof such as photos, price range or reviews?

Step 6: Put the words where they belong

  • Business name: only your real, registered or signage name. Adding keywords breaks Google's guidelines and risks suspension.
  • Primary and secondary categories: the most important place for "what you are".
  • Services or products: one short, plain description per item.
  • Business description: natural sentences about what you do and where, without links or promotions.
  • Website: page titles, headings and body text that mirror the way people speak; see Website SEO for a local business.
  • Reviews and replies: they often contain the exact words customers use, so reply naturally.

Check, then keep what works

After a month, look at your Business Profile's search terms and, if you have a website, Search Console queries. Which phrases bring views and calls? Which expected phrases are missing? Update your sheet, and see local rank tracking for how to measure movement. Remember that nobody can promise you a ranking for any phrase.

A worked example (illustrative)

Take a sweet shop in Indore. The owner's first list is "best sweet shop" and "mithai". After listening to customers and checking suggestions, the list becomes richer: "sev and namkeen wholesale", "mawa kachori near Rajwada", "order mithai for office Diwali gift", "sugar free sweets", "pure ghee laddu". Each phrase maps to something concrete: a product, a service, a photo, a Diwali post. This is an invented example to show how phrases turn into profile and website content.

Notice what changed. Instead of one generic phrase the owner now has several that reflect real demand: bulk orders for gifting, a local landmark, a dietary need. Each can become a product entry, a short post or a line on a gifting page.

A seasonal keyword calendar

Local demand follows the calendar, and so should your keyword list. Sketch a twelve-month view and write the phrases that will matter before each peak:

  • Before summer: AC service, cooler repair, water tank cleaning.
  • Before monsoon: waterproofing, leakage repair, raincoats, car wash and underbody coating.
  • Navratri to Diwali: sweets, gift hampers, tailoring, decor, cleaning services, home painting, jewellery.
  • Wedding season: banquet halls, caterers, photographers, mehendi, bridal makeup, tent and decor.
  • Exam and admission season: coaching, stationery, printing, hostels and PGs.
  • Winter: dry cleaning of woollens, gas geysers, heaters, health check-ups.

Update posts, photos and services a few weeks before each period so the right words are already on your profile when searches rise.

Turn questions into content

Every question you collect is a page or post waiting to be written. "How much does it cost?" can become a price-range section. "Do you come to my area?" becomes a service area list. "How long will it take?" becomes a timeline. A short FAQ on your website answering ten real questions will serve customers better than a long page of vague claims, and you can reuse the same answers in your Business Profile Q&A and in WhatsApp replies.

When you write, keep each answer honest and specific, avoid promising outcomes you cannot control, and add a way to contact you. For the website side, continue with website SEO for a local business, and use the business description generator and services generator to draft clean text you can edit.

Keyword research checklist

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Putting keywords into the business name.
  • Stuffing the description with a list of areas and services.
  • Targeting phrases for services you do not offer.
  • Ignoring Hinglish and Hindi spellings.
  • Building one near-identical page for every locality.
  • Trusting any tool's volume number as exact.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need paid tools for local keyword research?

No. Customer conversations, Google suggestions, People also ask and competitor profiles give a strong starting list. Paid tools can add volume estimates, but those are approximate.

Should I write keywords in Hindi or English?

Use the language your customers use. In many Indian cities that is a mix. Keep your main text natural in one language and include common alternates where they fit.

Is it okay to add "near me" to my description?

It is not necessary. Google already uses the searcher's location, so describe what you do and where you are in natural language instead.

How many keywords should I target?

Think in services, not a count. Cover each real service you offer clearly, and a short list of 20 to 30 phrases is plenty for most small businesses.
