Local SEO Reporting for Owners: What to Track and How to Read It

Good reporting is short, honest and tied to money. Here is a monthly one-page report for owners, using numbers you can find yourself, and a way to talk about them without jargon.

What a report is for

A report exists to help you make one or two decisions next month: where to spend time, what to keep doing and what to stop. If a report is thirty pages of charts that nobody acts on, it has failed, however pretty it looks. For a local business, the useful question is simple: are more of the right people finding us, contacting us and coming in?

Whether you handle this yourself, use a dashboard or work with an agency, insist on the same discipline. Every number should have a source, a date and a reason it matters. If a figure is unavailable, say so rather than estimating it silently.

The numbers worth tracking

Keep the list short. Three groups of numbers cover most local businesses:

  • Actions: phone calls, direction requests, website clicks and messages from your Google Business Profile, plus enquiries you log yourself from WhatsApp, walk-ins and forms.
  • Visibility: profile views and impressions, the search terms people used, and where available your map visibility by location (see local rank tracking), and website queries from Search Console.
  • Reputation: number of new reviews this month, average rating, percentage of reviews you replied to and the response time.

Add one business number the owner already knows, such as new customers or bookings this month. The point is to compare marketing signals with real outcomes, not to prove a correlation that does not exist.

Build a one-page monthly report

Use the same layout every month so that you can compare. A simple structure:

  • Headline: three sentences in plain language on what changed.
  • Numbers table: this month, last month, same month last year if you have it.
  • What we did: the two or three actions taken, with dates.
  • What we saw: honest observations, including things that got worse.
  • Next month: the one or two things to try, and who is responsible.

Keep a running log of changes you make: new services added, photos uploaded, review campaign started, hours changed. Without that log, you will not know why numbers moved.

Reading changes honestly

Numbers in a small business are noisy. A shop that gets 30 calls a month can easily see 24 one month and 36 the next without anything changing. Look at three to six months of trend before drawing conclusions, and expect seasonality: AC repair peaks before summer, sweets before Diwali, tutoring before exams, wedding services in the wedding season. Compare with the same period last year when you can.

Be careful with cause and effect. If calls rose after you added photos, that might be the photos, or the season, or a competitor closing. Treat each change as a hypothesis, keep the log, and repeat what keeps working. Nobody can promise that an action will move a ranking or a number.

Vanity metrics to ignore

  • Total impressions on their own, without calls or visits.
  • Rank for your own business name.
  • Follower counts that do not lead to enquiries.
  • A single tool's "score" treated as truth.
  • Keyword positions reported without location, device or date.

If you work with an agency

Ask for the same report in plain language, with data sources named. Ask what was done each month, how it ties to calls or enquiries, and what you own at the end: your Business Profile, website and accounts should always be in your name with you as owner. Be wary of promises of guaranteed first place, bundles of hundreds of directory submissions, or review packages. A good partner is comfortable explaining what did not work.

A sample report in plain words (illustrative)

Here is how a short headline might read for a clinic. The numbers are invented to show the style: "Calls from our Google profile were slightly higher than last month, and direction requests were similar. We added three new services and replied to all eight new reviews. The average rating is unchanged. We cannot yet tell whether the new services caused the extra calls, because the month also included a local health camp. Next month we will add photos of the waiting area and test Sunday morning hours."

Notice what is there: plain language, what was done, an honest statement of uncertainty, and a clear next step. Notice what is missing: bold promises, unexplained charts and a score.

Set targets that are about behaviour, not outcomes

You cannot control Google's ranking, but you can control habits. Good targets are "reply to every review within two days", "upload ten real photos each quarter", "log every enquiry's source" and "review numbers on the first working day of the month". Meet these and the numbers usually take care of themselves over time, although nobody can guarantee it.

Monthly reporting checklist

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Reporting only the numbers that look good.
  • Changing the format every month.
  • Treating a short-term dip as a disaster.
  • Ignoring seasonality.
  • Not recording what you changed.
  • Letting an agency hold the only login.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should an owner review local SEO numbers?

Monthly is enough for most small businesses. Check reviews weekly because customers expect timely replies, and review trends over three to six months.

Which single number matters most?

Customer actions: calls, direction requests and enquiries. Visibility only matters if it leads to them.

Can I get a report automatically?

Paid dashboards can pull Business Profile and Search Console data after you connect your accounts. Where data is not available, a good report says so. Our pricing page lists what each plan includes.

What is a good rating target?

There is no magic number. Focus on genuine reviews, replying to all of them and fixing the causes of low ratings; never filter or buy reviews.
