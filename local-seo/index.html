Local SEO Basics

Local SEO for small businesses: the basics

"Local SEO" gets used loosely. Here's what it actually covers, how it's different from general SEO, and which basics are worth getting right before anything more advanced.

Local SEO vs. regular SEO

Regular SEO is about ranking a website for a topic, regardless of location. Local SEO is about ranking for searches that have a local intent — either because the searcher typed a location ("electrician in Andheri") or because Google inferred one from their device.

For most local businesses, the Google Business Profile matters at least as much as the website itself, since a large share of local searches are answered directly in the Maps/local pack results without a click-through.

The three pillars of local SEO

  • Google Business Profile: completeness, accuracy, category, and verification status.
  • Citations & consistency: your business name, address and phone number appearing the same way across your website and any directories or listings.
  • Reviews & reputation: both the number and recency of reviews, and how you respond to them.
A simple way to think about it: local SEO is less about "beating an algorithm" and more about making sure Google — and your customers — have accurate, consistent information about your business everywhere it appears.

A realistic starting checklist

  1. Confirm your Google Business Profile is claimed, verified, and accurate
  2. Check that your name, address and phone number match exactly on your website
  3. List your business on 2–3 relevant, reputable directories with the same details
  4. Make sure your website mentions your service area and city clearly, in plain language
  5. Start asking recent customers for genuine reviews, one at a time

None of this guarantees a specific ranking — but skipping these basics is one of the most common reasons a business stays invisible regardless of anything else it tries.
