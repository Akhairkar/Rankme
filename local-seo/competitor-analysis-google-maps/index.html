Competitor Analysis on Google Maps for Local Businesses

You do not need spy tools. Studying the three or four businesses your customers actually compare you with shows you what to fix first, and where you can be genuinely different.

Who your competitors really are

Your Google Maps competitors are not always the businesses you think of as rivals. They are the profiles that appear when your customer searches. A boutique hotel competes with other stays in the same lane, but also with guest houses and homestays that show in the same results. A tutor competes with coaching institutes, app-based classes and the teacher down the road.

Start by searching five phrases your customers would type, from a phone, at the place you want to win customers from. Write down the businesses that show in the top results more than once. Those are your real competitors for this map, even if they are larger, smaller or different from you in price.

Build a simple comparison table

Open each competitor's profile and note facts in a sheet. Use the same columns for everyone, including yourself:

Read their reviews like market research

Competitor reviews are a free survey of your customers. Read the last 20 to 30 of each, including the one-star and three-star ones. Mark the topics that come up again and again, such as waiting time, parking, hygiene, staff behaviour, price transparency, delivery delays or after-sales support.

Praise points tell you what customers value in this category. Complaints tell you where you can be better. If three competitors get repeated complaints about unanswered phone calls and you reply within an hour on WhatsApp, that is a real advantage. Do not copy private details or reproduce reviews on your own website.

Find gaps you can fill honestly

  • A service nobody lists clearly, such as Sunday appointments, home visits or same-day delivery.
  • A neighbourhood that competitors ignore but your customers come from.
  • Photos that actually show the shop, team or work, when rivals use stock or logos.
  • Specific services with prices, when competitors have a vague description.
  • A faster response to calls and WhatsApp enquiries.
  • More recent reviews if theirs are old.

A gap is only useful if you can serve it. Do not add services you do not provide just to match a competitor.

Check the website and citations quickly

Open the competitor's website on your phone. Is it fast? Does it have clear service pages, an address and a phone number? Does it explain prices or the process? Then search their business name plus city and see on which directories they are listed. The citation checker can help you keep a tally. You are not trying to copy everything, only noticing which basics they have covered that you have not.

What not to do

  • Do not post fake negative reviews about a competitor or ask others to; it is against Google's policies and can be illegal.
  • Do not report a competitor's profile as fake unless it truly violates policy. Genuine reports are fine, vengeful ones are not.
  • Do not copy photos, text or reviews.
  • Do not change the name of your listing to look like theirs.
  • Do not assume bigger means better. A competitor with 2,000 reviews may be weaker in your lane.

Turn findings into a 30-day plan

Choose three actions at most. For example: add six services with short descriptions, upload fifteen real photos, and set up a review request message you send to every customer. Write the date, run the check again after a month and update your table. Competitors change too, so repeat this exercise every quarter. A worked example (illustrative)

Suppose you run a ladies' salon in Jaipur's Vaishali Nagar. Searching "bridal makeup near me" and "hair spa in Vaishali Nagar" shows four salons repeatedly. Filling the sheet reveals that two of them have many reviews but few photos from the last year, one lists no prices, and one is open until 9 pm while the others close at 7. This is an invented scenario to show the method, not real data.

Your honest conclusions might be: you can compete on fresh photos, you can publish starting prices for the top five services, and you can test whether late-evening appointments bring bookings, if your staff can support it. You would not conclude that you need to match the largest salon's review count tomorrow, because that cannot be done honestly overnight.

Notice that every conclusion is an action you control. That is the test of a good competitor analysis: it ends with a short to-do list for your own profile, not with a feeling of being behind.

Questions to ask your own team

Your staff hear things competitors never see. Ask your counter staff and technicians once a month: which rival do customers mention by name? What do people say they liked there? What do they ask you for that we do not offer? Which enquiries did we lose, and why? Write the answers next to your sheet. A single repeated sentence from customers, such as "they gave a written estimate", can be more valuable than a page of audit data.

Competitor analysis checklist

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Comparing yourself only with the biggest brand.
  • Copying a competitor's categories without checking they fit you.
  • Reading ratings but ignoring how recent the reviews are.
  • Trying to fix everything at once.
  • Engaging in fake reviews or false reports.
  • Never repeating the analysis.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many competitors should I analyse?

Three to five is enough. More becomes hard to maintain, and fewer may miss the business your customer actually compares you with.

Can I see which keywords a competitor ranks for?

Not exactly from Google Maps. You can see their categories, services, reviews and website text, and test searches yourself. Paid tools estimate keywords but those are approximations.

Is it okay to read competitor reviews?

Yes, they are public. Use them to understand customers, but do not copy them or contact reviewers.

What if my competitor has far more reviews?

Focus on what you can control: recent, genuine reviews, replies, completeness and service. Review count is only one part of prominence, and you should never buy reviews to catch up.
